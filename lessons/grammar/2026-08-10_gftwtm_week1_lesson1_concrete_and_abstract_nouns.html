Grammar for the Well-Trained Mind • Week 1, Lesson 1

The Game of Tangible & Intangible Nouns

Wherein Rama Lama, Slush Puppy, and Dilly Engage in a Witty Parlor Game of Concrete and Abstract Nouns

Chapter I: The Parlor Game on the Flagstone Porch

The afternoon breeze blew gently across the flagstone porch outside Rama Lama’s study, carrying the sweet scent of mountain pine and dried clover. Seated upon a woven carpet around a low wooden table were three friends: Rama Lama, the wise bighorn sheep; Slush Puppy, the clever husky trail leader; and Dilly, the thoughtful armadillo. Between them rested a velvet pouch filled with polished wooden tiles, each carved with a single English word.

"Welcome, my esteemed companions," began Rama Lama in his articulate, calm cadence, adjusting his turquoise necklace. "Today, we shall test our wits with the most fundamental building block of our speech: the noun. As you well know, a noun is a word that names a person, a place, a thing, or an idea."

Slush Puppy tilted his head, his sharp blue-grey eyes twinkling with humor. "That sounds simple enough, Rama Lama! A husky is a person—or rather, a fine canine fellow; the trail is a place; my compass is a thing; and my plan to find lunch is an idea!"

Dilly

"Ah, but Slush Puppy," noted Dilly carefully, adjusting her calipers, "the true subtlety lies in dividing our nouns into two great realms: concrete nouns and abstract nouns. Let us make certain our rules are clear before we draw the first tile!"

Grammar Rules: Concrete vs. Abstract Nouns

  • • Noun Definition: A word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.
  • • Concrete Noun: Names something that can be experienced through one or more of the five senses (seen, touched, heard, smelled, or tasted). Examples: pinecone, whistle, stone, fur, stream.
  • • Abstract Noun: Names an idea, quality, emotion, or state of being that cannot be perceived directly by the physical senses. Examples: courage, curiosity, patience, loyalty, wisdom.

Chapter II: The Physical & The Paws

Rama Lama reached into the velvet pouch and drew forth the first wooden tile. "Our first tile bears the word: WHISTLE. Slush Puppy, how do you categorize this noun?"

Slush Puppy

"A whistle! Why, that is as concrete as the granite ledge on Sentinel Peak! I can hold a silver brass whistle in my paw; I can see it gleam in the sun; and when I blow into it, the piercing sound makes everyone's ears perk up! Since I can see, touch, and hear it, whistle is undeniably a concrete noun!"

"Spot on, Slush Puppy," smiled Rama Lama. "Any noun that registers upon your five senses—whether it be the rough texture of a pinecone, the cold splash of a stream, or the sharp scent of mint—is concrete."

Dilly carefully scribbled in her ledger. "So if I can press my snout against it, or hear its echo in the valley, it belongs to the physical world of concrete nouns."

Slush Puppy

"Indeed! Even if I cannot bite it—such as a cloud or a star—it is still concrete because my eyes can see it twinkling in the sky!"

❧ • ❧ • ❧

Chapter III: The Realm of Pure Thought

Rama Lama reached into the velvet pouch once more and drew out a second tile. "Our second word is: COURAGE. Dilly, what say you?"

Dilly tapped her claw thoughtfully against her chin. "Hmm... courage. Can I hold a cup of courage in my claws? No. Can I see the color of courage directly under a magnifying glass? No. Courage is a quality of the heart—a brave state of mind when facing danger. Therefore, courage is an abstract noun!"

Slush Puppy

"Hold on a moment, Dilly!" challenged Slush Puppy playfully, wagging his tail. "When we crossed the stormy ravine last spring, I felt my fur stand on end, my heart thudding like a drum! Surely I felt courage!"

Rama Lama

"Ah, a clever distinction, Slush Puppy! Your fur and your heart are concrete things you can feel with your body. But courage itself is the invisible idea and noble quality that inspired your actions. You cannot put courage in a wheelbarrow or weigh it on a scale!"

Slush Puppy chuckled. "Fair point, Rama Lama! A wheelbarrow full of courage would certainly save us some trouble on difficult climbs!"

Abstract Nouns Classification Guide

  • • Qualities: honesty, bravery, wisdom, intelligence, grace.
  • • Emotions: joy, sorrow, fear, delight, anger.
  • • Relationships & States: friendship, freedom, peace, youth, victory.
  • • Concepts & Ideas: truth, time, justice, geometry, honor.
❧ • ❧ • ❧

Chapter IV: The Witty Match & Synthesis

"Now," said Rama Lama, his dark eyes shining with amusement, "let us play the rapid match round! I shall name a concrete noun, and you two must pair it with a corresponding abstract noun that it brings to mind."

Rama Lama called out: "LANTERN!"

Dilly

"Lantern is concrete—glass and metal! My abstract pair is HOPE, for a lantern guides us through the dark night!"

Rama Lama called out: "MAP!"

Slush Puppy

"Map is concrete paper and ink! My abstract pair is KNOWLEDGE, for a map reveals the true way ahead!"

Rama Lama called out: "HANDSHAKE!"

Dilly & Slush Puppy (together)

"Handshake is concrete motion and touch! Its abstract pair is FRIENDSHIP!"

Rama Lama nodded in warm approval. "Splendidly done, my friends! You have shown that while concrete nouns form the physical world around us, abstract nouns give meaning, purpose, and beauty to our thoughts."

As the afternoon sun dipped low over the mountains, the three companions packed away their wooden tiles, well-pleased with their mastery of the spoken word.
