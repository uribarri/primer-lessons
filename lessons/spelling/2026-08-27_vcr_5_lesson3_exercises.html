Dictionary Listings & Study Notes

TRANS (from the Latin word trans meaning “across, through”)

Familiar Words with Root TRANS

  • • transform

Challenge Words with Root TRANS

  • • transient
  • • translucent
  • • transgress
1. transact (trăn săkt´) [Latin agere]
v. To carry on or conduct business across parties.
Merchants gather downtown to transact business with regional buyers.
2. transfer (trăns fûr´)
v. To move or pass from one place, person, or position to another.
She will transfer to the new high school in the fall semester.
3. transfusion (trăns fyū´ zhən)
n. The process of transferring blood or plasma into a person’s bloodstream.
The injured hiker received a blood transfusion at the hospital.
4. transmit (trăns mĭt´)
v. To pass along, broadcast, or send from one person or place to another.
Radio towers transmit signals to receivers across the state.

Nota Bene

The prefix inter- means between or among, while trans- means across or through. Notice how inter-, trans-, and intra- (meaning within) form contrasting directional prefixes.

INTER (from the Latin word inter meaning “between, among”)

Familiar Words with Root INTER

  • • international
  • • intermediate

Challenge Words with Root INTER

  • • intercede
  • • interject
  • • interlocutor
5. interactive (ĭn tər ăk´ tĭv)
adj. Involving active continuous dialogue or action between two or more people or things.
The museum features interactive displays that children can touch and explore.
6. interfere (ĭn tər fēr´)
v. To come between so as to block, impede, or hinder action.
Loud music should not interfere with student studying.
7. intermittent (ĭn tər mĭt´ nt)
adj. Stopping and starting at intervals; periodic rather than continuous.
Intermittent rain showers continued throughout the afternoon.
8. intersect (ĭn tər sĕkt´)
v. To cross or cut through one another.
Main Street and Grand Avenue intersect at the clock tower.
9. interval (ĭn´ tər vəl)
n. A period of time between two events, or space between two objects.
There was a brief interval of silence before the applause began.
